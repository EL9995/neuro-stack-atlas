Stack Builder review

A round of changes to the Stack builder is ready but not published. Before it goes live, we need a decision from legal and ethics on one question: what the builder should do when someone enters a dose far above the normal range.

October 5, 2026 · Status: built and tested on Eric's machine, not pushed to the live site

Decision needed: extreme doses Legal + ethics

Example: someone adds L-Tyrosine at 10,000 mg. The typical range on the site is 500–2,000 mg per dose. Should the builder allow it, warn harder, or stop it?

What happens today

Dose box
No upper limit. Any number can be typed in.
Above typical range
Flagged To review “Above the typical 500–2,000 mg per dose.” 10,000 mg gets exactly the same flag as 3,000 mg.
Above upper limit
Only 6 supplements have one in our data (caffeine 400 mg, vitamin C 2,000 mg, B6 100 mg, D3 4,000 IU, omega-3 5,000 mg, copper 10 mg), plus magnesium, zinc and iron as groups. Going over it is flagged Serious.
Saving
Any “to review” or “serious” flag means the person must tick “I've read the warnings” before saving. Nothing is ever blocked.
Tracker
A saved stack becomes a daily checklist, so a 10,000 mg dose would show up there as part of the plan.
Disclaimers
“For learning only, not medical advice” in the footer and at the top of the home page; the save step adds “Check with a doctor or pharmacist first, especially if you take medication.”

Options

A

Keep as is: warn, allow

Least friction. But 10,000 mg looks no different from 3,000 mg, and the Tracker then presents the dose as a plan.

B

Warn harder as the dose climbs

Above the typical range: “to review” (as now). Far above it (for example more than 2× the top of the range) or above an upper limit: “serious”, with a plain message and the doctor reminder. Still saveable after acknowledging.

C

B, plus block saving above a hard ceiling

Same tiers as B, but a stack can't be saved or tracked while any dose is over a hard ceiling. The person can still type it and see the warning, so we teach instead of silently refusing; they just can't turn it into a plan. Only works where we have a sourced ceiling.

D

Cap the dose box

The input refuses numbers over a ceiling. Simplest to explain, but it hides the reason from the person, and they may just take the dose without the tool.

Why C: it keeps the harm-reduction value (people see why the dose is a problem) without the site ever presenting an unsafe amount as a saved plan or a daily checklist item.

Questions for legal and ethics

  1. Is it acceptable for a saved stack, or the Tracker checklist, to show a dose above a published upper limit?
  2. Is ticking “I've read the warnings” enough of an acknowledgement for “serious” items, or should some items block saving?
  3. Where would hard ceilings come from? Our data has typical ranges for all 65 supplements but upper limits for only 6 (plus 3 mineral groups). Each new ceiling is a factual claim that needs a source.
  4. What may a blocking message say without reading as medical advice? For example: “This is above the [source] upper limit. The builder won't save it.”

What changed in this round

All in the Stack builder. Nothing else on the site changed.

  1. 01Steps open one at a time

    New visitors see step 1; each step ends with “Next”. “Take the tour” walks through step by step; “Skip the tour” opens everything.

  2. 02Every step folds to one line

    Click a step to fold it to a summary (“8 supplements…”, “2 to review · 1 minor”, “Not saved yet”). Expand all / Collapse all at the top.

  3. 03Check comes before the timeline

    New order: name, day, add, check, timeline, save. Nobody reaches the schedule without passing the warnings.

  4. 04New “Overlap” checks

    Several raw materials for one neurotransmitter, raw materials that skip the body's slow step (Mucuna, 5-HTP), and 4+ supplements piled on one pathway. Phenylalanine + Tyrosine + Mucuna now gets a “to review” alert.

  5. 05Foundations + recovery

    A browse tab above the neurotransmitters in step 3, a matching row in the timeline, and its own color (lime). Built from the site's existing Foundations list.

  6. 06Compact lists

    “In this stack” is grouped by pathway and folds; folded groups still show “! 1 to check”. Suggestions are folded behind one heading and shown as rows, so nothing is pushed at people.

  7. 07Timeline grouped by pathway

    Pathway rows are the main view; click one to see and drag its supplements.

  8. 08Simpler dose and time entry

    Times are set automatically and moved on the timeline only. Doses have − / + buttons that step in round amounts sized to each supplement.

New wording that makes a claim

Marked DRAFT in the code. Everything else new is interface text (“Next”, “Expand all”, “Add to stack”).
